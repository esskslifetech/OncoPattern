# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be precise, be civil, assume competence. This repository is about measurement:
claims are checked against certificates, fixtures and published references, and
a correction is a contribution rather than a defeat.

## Expected

- **Argue with evidence.** If you disagree with a threshold, a model or a
  figure, say what it should be and what you measured.
- **Keep criticism on the code.** "This detector's density denominator is per
  field, not per mm²" is useful; "this detector is nonsense" is not.
- **Respect provenance.** The patent and literature fixtures carry API
  responses as evidence. Do not alter them to fit an argument.
- **Acknowledge uncertainty.** "I could not reproduce this" is a valid and
  valuable report.

## Not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, demeaning comments, or unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Discriminatory language or imagery relating to identity, experience or
  background.
- Publishing another person's private information without permission.
- Dismissing a report because the reporter is new to the project.
- Presenting another person's work as your own, including unattributed
  reproduction of the platform's code or its published reference base.

## Scope

This applies to issues, pull requests, commit messages, discussions, and any
space where someone is representing this project.

## Enforcement

Report a problem through GitHub's private vulnerability reporting on this
repository, or by opening an issue if the matter is public and non-sensitive.
Maintainers will review, may remove content, and may block accounts. Reports are
handled confidentially; the reporter's identity is not disclosed without their
consent.
